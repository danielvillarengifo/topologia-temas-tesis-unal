We study whether the master's and doctoral theses in Statistics and Mathematics at the Universidad Nacional de Colombia changed in topics, shape and vocabulary from 2023 onward. The corpus is 910 abstracts and, for 396 theses, also the running text (the prose of the PDF, without tables or formulas). We compare windows of years: 2018-2021 against 2023-2026, with generative artificial intelligence (AI) in between, and, as a control, two earlier windows against each other. In self-organizing maps trained with 20 seeds, the theses of 2023-2026 are distributed differently from those of 2018-2021 in 14 of 20 maps with one text representation and in 7 of 20 with the other; in the control, in 3 and in 1. A multilayer perceptron separates those two windows with an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.70; in the control the AUC is 0.49 (0.5 is chance). Machine learning theses go from 2% to 19% of the total. Seen as a point cloud, recent theses are more alike: the mean distance between two theses fell by 2.7% with one representation and 1.7% with the other, a larger drop than random reassignment of theses to windows produces (p = 0.0025 and 0.0005). Persistent homology also finds that the cycles of that cloud (closed chains of similar theses) differ between the two windows; once this contraction is discounted, that evidence is weak. In the control no shape test survives the multiplicity adjustment. In the running text, 323 of 1,991 words rise in frequency between 2019-2021 and 2023-2026, while in the control none changes; with all theses cut to the same length, 96 rise. All comparisons are temporal: they do not isolate the effect of AI.
